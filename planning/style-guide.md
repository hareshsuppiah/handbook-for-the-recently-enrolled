# Editorial style guide

## Voice

Write like an experienced, thoughtful supervisor who respects the reader. Be direct, concrete, intellectually serious, and honest about uncertainty.

Use Australian/British spelling. Explain technical terms when first needed. Distinguish evidence, practical convention, local policy, judgement, and unresolved questions.

## Avoid

- Generic encouragement or claims that research is a journey.
- Canned AI transitions and repetitive `not X but Y` constructions.
- Excessive em dashes, slogans, fake quotations, or invented anecdotes.
- Treating one discipline, method, institution, or jurisdiction as universal.
- Presenting templates as substitutes for expertise or judgement.
- Unsupported thresholds or claims of completeness.

## Page language

- Reader-facing navigation: **Suggest an improvement**, **I'm stuck**, **Before you ask your supervisor**, **Local rules apply**, **Evidence required**, and **Recovery procedure**.
- Use **On this page** for the right-hand chapter contents.
- Label content state as **Draft**, **Review ready**, or **Verified**.
- Time-sensitive pages show **Last reviewed** and **Review by**.

## Chapter pattern

Lead with the reader's question and a direct answer. Then explain the judgement, workflow, decision rules, example, failure modes, checklist or template, supervisor preparation, escalation, variation, and evidence.
