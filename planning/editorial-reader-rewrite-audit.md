# Reader-focused editorial audit

**Planned:** 28 August 2026  
**Applied and verified:** 30 August 2026 on `main`  
**Scope:** all 36 handbook chapters and their connected checklists, templates and stuck pathways

**Corrective title pass, 30 August 2026:** The first rewrite used a repetitive question format for most chapter titles. That format has been withdrawn. The current titles are short, topic-led labels designed to scan like a book contents page. Recurring project-management language in section headings was also replaced with ordinary teaching language. The pass used the `no-ai-slop` editing rules followed by the local LLM-cliche highlighter; functional checklists, definitions and question prompts were reviewed as structured teaching material rather than treated as prose defects.

## Preservation rule

The rewrite retains every factual and instructional claim; citation and the claim it supports; ethics, privacy, consent, integrity, safety and governance boundary; institutional, jurisdictional, disciplinary and methodological caveat; escalation criterion; stop condition; practical-resource link; and distinction needed to prevent overclaiming. Structural cuts are limited to repeated question inventories and project-facing scaffolding. Their useful questions move into situation-led openings, decision headings or next actions. No evidence-bearing paragraph is cut merely to lighten the prose.

For each chapter, the protected inventory is recoverable from the pre-rewrite Git version and checked after editing by comparing citation keys, internal links, callouts containing stop or escalation language, and locally bounded guidance. `planning/requirements-checklist.csv` remains the topic and resource authority.

## Checklist, template and stuck-pathway title audit

Resource titles are retained when they already name the decision or artefact plainly. Their titles are checked against the renamed chapters and navigation during each Part pass. Abstract index labels in navigation become **Checklists for important pause points**, **Templates you can use**, and **Find a way forward when you’re stuck**. Renaming a resource file is avoided unless the reader-facing title is misleading, so existing chapter links remain stable. Every retained or revised resource title is recorded in the final diff and checked against the 53 `RES-` rows in the requirement ledger.

## Ambiguities reserved for Haresh

- The main title and subtitle remain unchanged under DEC-001 and DEC-002.
- “Doctorate” remains in chapter 1 because the handbook includes more than one doctoral form; “PhD” remains searchable in the opening and body.
- Chapter 18 keeps authorship in the title because it is both the likely reader search term and a consequential decision.
