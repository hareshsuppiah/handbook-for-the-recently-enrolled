# Reader audit: running, writing, recovering and finishing the doctorate

Date: 8 September 2026. Scope: complete source text of chapters 25–36, all 13 `stuck/` pages, `support.qmd` and `resources.qmd` (27 pages). This is the late-section component of the whole-book audit. The parent audit owns external benchmarking, visual selection, the contribution workflow, shared interaction, build and publication verification.

## Basis and limits

The audit used the graduate-research-reader-editor skill, its reader-friendly style model and `planning/style-guide.md`. Findings were sent to the coordinating agent before editing. The source was already stronger than a generic draft: every chapter had a recognisable opening, useful requirements and important boundaries. The recurring weakness was the move from that opening into lists of procedural terms without enough examples or help for a reader whose circumstances differ from a full-time computational project.

The revisions add editorial examples and practical prompts rather than new research findings. Invented model statements are identified as examples; they must not be copied as evidence about a real project. No new universal policy, clinical threshold, causal finding or guaranteed examination/career outcome was introduced. Existing external source and university-policy links were preserved. Their live accuracy is not established by this component audit; the main external scan is recorded separately. `last-reviewed` records the editorial review date, not a claim that every cited source has been independently revalidated today.

## Preservation and verification

Before edits, a temporary snapshot of all 27 sources was saved for comparison. The revised sources preserve all 23 original citation keys across 41 citation occurrences, all 94 explicit anchor occurrences and all 173 original prose/resource-link occurrences. No existing heading was renamed in this component. The new local link destinations resolve, and the four `<details>/<summary>` examples have balanced markup. Claims and protected boundaries were also reviewed in their surrounding passages: counts alone cannot establish that their meaning survived.

The two substantive safety clarifications are intentional: repeated search results no longer imply a stopping rule for protocol-led reviews, and a reviewer cannot waive a critical requirement outside their authority. Safe-to-repeat conditions were added to technical troubleshooting. Existing incident, wellbeing, privacy, consent, ethics, authorship, examination and local-policy boundaries were retained. The only removed repeated requirement was the second instruction to retain the same upload confirmation; the first remains in place.

`python3 scripts/verify_book.py` passed after the edits, reporting 36 chapters and 113 rendered HTML pages. At that point `_site` was still the prior build, so this check demonstrates source-contract preservation and the existing rendered site's checks; it does not establish that the new pages were rendered or usable in the final interface.

A subsequent occurrence-level comparison passed for every original citation, explicit anchor and prose/resource link. A verifier rerun during the shared build work found no HTML files in `_site` and therefore failed its rendered-site checks. The final completed render must be checked again; the earlier cached-site pass must not be reported as final build validation.

Four worked-reveal pages were converted with `/opt/homebrew/bin/pandoc`; all four retained native details and summary elements. The Quarto wrapper's hardware probe was blocked by the sandbox (`sysctl: Operation not permitted`), so direct Pandoc was used only for this bounded markup check. The coordinating agent was informed that the full Quarto render and browser checks remain required.
