# Title review

Date: 28 August 2026  
Status: awaiting Haresh's scope decision

## The naming problem

“Recently enrolled” is memorable and preserves the uncanny-handbook joke, but it does not identify graduate research on its own. “Enrolled” could describe an undergraduate, coursework student or almost any learner. Replacing it with “admitted” does not solve that problem. “Candidate” and “candidature” are also imperfect because their formal meaning varies between systems and may apply only after confirmation.

## Recommended options

### 1. The PhD Operating Manual

Subtitle: **A Handbook for the Recently Enrolled**

Best when the primary audience is explicitly PhD students. It is clear, memorable and searchable, and it keeps “Recently Enrolled” as the distinctive wink rather than asking it to identify the audience.

### 2. The Graduate Research Operating Manual

Subtitle: **A Handbook for the Recently Enrolled**

Best when PhD and research-master's students should have equal prominence. It is accurate in Australian and British usage, although slightly more institutional and less globally immediate than “PhD”.

### 3. The Handbook for New Graduate Researchers

Subtitle: **An Operating Manual from Enrolment to Independence**

The clearest literal option, but less distinctive and less compatible with the existing cover concept.

## Recommendation

Use **The PhD Operating Manual: A Handbook for the Recently Enrolled** unless equal title-level prominence for research-master's students is essential. The scope statement can still explain that much of the guidance also applies to research-master's students.

Do not change the cover, repository name, metadata, citations or 36 chapters until this choice is locked. Once selected, change all naming surfaces together and record a superseding decision in `planning/decision-log.md`.
