# Content expansion review

Reviewed: 31 August 2026

This is an editorial gap review, not a promise to add every topic. A topic earns space when it helps a graduate researcher make a recurring decision that the current handbook does not yet handle well. Local rules, specialist methods and community authority still take priority where they apply.

## How this review was done

The review compared the 36 chapter titles, chapter text, checklists and templates against recurring doctoral situations. Targeted searches of the current book covered funding and costs, partner agreements, intellectual property, translation, accessibility, fieldwork and researcher safety, cultural governance, different doctoral forms, personal disruption and career transition.

The evidence search for contingency planning used official research, quality-assurance and government guidance plus a peer-reviewed systematic review. Sources were included only when the original page, PDF or DOI metadata could be checked. Commercial risk templates and unverified summaries were excluded.

This was a targeted editorial search rather than a systematic review. It can show that a gap is plausible; it cannot establish how often every graduate researcher encounters it.

## Addition completed in this pass

Chapter 26 now explains how to write a contingency that can be activated. It names the protected outcome, trigger, fallback, decision owner, required authority, prerequisites, review date and evidence that the fallback works. The linked risk-and-issues register now contains a contingency card and a completed recruitment example.

The chapter covers delayed access, recruitment shortfall, equipment or data loss, supervisor absence, failed analysis assumptions, personal disruption and compressed timelines. It keeps ethics, consent, participant welfare, data controls and safety outside the trade-offs a project can make for speed.

## Topics that already have a substantive home

These appeared in the scan but do not currently justify a new chapter:

- communicating beyond a paper is covered in Chapter 30;
- career and post-candidature transition are covered in Chapter 36;
- qualitative, archival and practice-based provenance is covered in Chapters 20 and 21;
- personal capacity and wellbeing escalation are covered in Chapters 26 and 32; and
- conference or journal submission checks are covered in Chapter 30 and the manuscript-submission checklist.

The better next move is to test those sections with readers from different project types before adding more navigation.

## Next editorial check

The next expansion pass should begin with budget and partner-agreement coverage because both affect feasibility before substantial work begins. Fieldwork safety, language and accessibility should then be reviewed with people who use those practices. Community and Indigenous governance material should wait for an appropriate human-led and community-authorised process.
